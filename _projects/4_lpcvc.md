## Why it is needed

Deciding whether an image was generated by AI is something a phone increasingly needs to answer on its own, for provenance, for moderation, or because the user wants to know. Existing detectors either need server-scale compute, or they fail on diffusion generators they were not trained on.

## What I did

A two-stage vision-language detector built on **Qwen2-VL-2B (LoRA)**. It runs inside a hard budget of 500 tokens per stage on a **Snapdragon 8 Gen 5**.

- **Stage 1** looks at the image and writes free-text reasoning over eight visual criteria.
- **Stage 2** reads only that text, never the image, and outputs a JSON decision.

Dropping the image in Stage 2 removes a second pass through the vision encoder, which is most of the latency. It also leaves Stage 1's description as something a person can read, so a wrong answer can be understood instead of just observed.

I added an auxiliary classification head next to the generative output.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/projects/lpcvc_two_stage.svg" class="img-fluid rounded" %}

<div class="caption">Stage 1 reasons about the image in free text. Stage 2 reads only that text and produces the JSON decision. Dropping the image between stages removes the second vision-encoder pass and keeps the reasoning readable.</div>

**Getting it onto the phone.** The LoRA model is quantized to W4A16 with Qualcomm AIMET and exported as a QNN binary. 4-bit weights with 16-bit activations is the setting that survives here. The language head copes with heavy weight quantization. The activations do not.

## Result

- **1st place, LPCVC 2026 Track 3** at CVPR 2026
- Official rubric score **0.827 at 30.48 TPS**
- The auxiliary classification head added **+2.08 pp** on the 26,033-image Chameleon benchmark

Code, LoRA adapter and training dataset are released: [Ganzooo/LPCVC2026-Track3-OptimAI-1st](https://github.com/Ganzooo/LPCVC2026-Track3-OptimAI-1st) (MIT).
